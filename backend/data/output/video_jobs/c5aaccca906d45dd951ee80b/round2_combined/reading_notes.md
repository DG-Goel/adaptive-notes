## openness

- Opening an economy means you can buy goods both at home and abroad, requiring comparison of relative prices of domestic and foreign goods.
- The real exchange rate is the relative price of goods at home versus abroad, expressed in a common currency.

## real exchange rate

- When the real exchange rate rises, it is called a real appreciation, meaning domestic goods become more expensive relative to foreign goods.
- When the real exchange rate falls, it is called a real depreciation, meaning domestic goods become cheaper relative to foreign goods.
- When the real exchange rate rises, U.S. goods become more expensive relative to foreign goods, which reduces foreign demand for U.S. exports.
- If an important trading partner's output and income increase, its consumption of goods—including imports—rises, boosting demand for the partner’s exports.
- China is reopening, creating a large domestic boom.
- U.S. exports are decreasing as the real exchange rate rises.
