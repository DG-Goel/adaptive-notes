## Mandela Fleming model

- A framework that connects monetary policies in different countries to exchange‑rate movements and examines how those movements affect short‑run real activity.
- The price of one currency expressed in terms of another; the model studies how monetary policy differences across countries move the exchange rate and influence aggregate demand.

## net exports

- The component of aggregate demand that represents the difference between a country's exports and imports; in the model it is treated as a function of output and domestic income.
- Net exports increase when foreign (rest‑of‑the‑world) income rises because foreign demand for domestically produced goods rises.
- When the real exchange rate rises, domestic goods become more expensive relative to foreign goods, causing exports to fall and imports to rise, so net exports decline.

## exchange rate appreciation

- An increase in the domestic interest rate leads to a higher exchange rate, meaning the domestic currency becomes more expensive.
- When the domestic interest rate is increased, the exchange rate appreciates.
- If the exchange rate is kept fixed and the currency appreciates today, it is expected to depreciate in the next period.

## Interest rate parity

- Compensate the interest rate differential with an expected depreciation of the currency that is paying a higher interest rate.
- When fixing the expected exchange rate, the only way to do that is by appreciating the currency today so it can be expected to depreciate in the future.

## Currency appreciation and return convergence
