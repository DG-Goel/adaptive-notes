## net exports

- Net exports is a function of output, domestic income, and imports; it declines when output or domestic income rise because more of the demand is satisfied by foreign goods.
- Equilibrium in the goods market is when output equals total demand, including domestic demand and net exports.
- Net exports declines as domestic output increases.
- Net exports declines as domestic income rises because imports increase.
- Net exports increase when foreign (rest‑of‑the‑world) income rises and decline when the real exchange rate rises.
- A higher real exchange rate makes domestic goods more expensive relative to foreign goods, reducing exports and increasing imports.
- When the rest‑of‑the‑world’s income rises, its demand for domestically produced goods rises, so our exports increase.

## Expansionary fiscal policy

- An expansionary fiscal policy that does not change the interest rate has no effect on the exchange rate. Fiscal policy that raises output without affecting interest rates does not cause currency appreciation. In an open economy, a rise in output from expansionary fiscal policy can lead to currency appreciation only if it influences interest rates. When fiscal expansion leads investors to expect the central bank to raise interest rates, the currency tends to appreciate.
- Appreciation leads to net exports decline.
