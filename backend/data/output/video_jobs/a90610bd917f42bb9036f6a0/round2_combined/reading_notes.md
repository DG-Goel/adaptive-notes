## exchange rate

- The exchange rate moves in response to different monetary policies in different countries and influences aggregate demand and output in the very short run.
- The Mandela Fleming model connects monetary policies across countries to exchange‑rate movements and examines how those movements affect real activity in the short run.
- In the short run the IS‑LM framework can be applied with nominal prices fixed, extending it to an open economy by adding the exchange rate as a variable.
- Net exports are a function of output and domestic income.
- Domestic demand includes both domestic consumption and net exports.
- Net exports decrease when domestic income rises because imports increase.

## net exports

- Net exports increase when the income of the rest of the world rises because foreign demand for domestically produced goods goes up.
- When the real exchange rate rises, domestic goods become more expensive relative to foreign goods, causing exports to decline and imports to increase.
- Net exports rise when foreign income rises.
- A higher real exchange rate reduces net exports.
